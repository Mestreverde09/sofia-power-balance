How the model works and where the numbers come from
  • This is a simplified teaching model for one day, in half-hour steps. Real grids also trade power with neighbouring countries. Here, any shortfall counts as "lights out".
  • Demand: winter peak about 6.6 GW, summer peak about 5 GW. These are rounded estimates of Bulgaria's daily curve. Check the real curve at eso.bg.
  • Order of use: nuclear runs flat, then solar and wind. Spare power charges batteries, and anything left over is wasted. When power runs short, the model uses batteries, then gas, then coal. Hydro comes last, for the peaks, because the dams only hold enough water for 6 hours at full power.
  • CO₂: lignite coal 1.1 t per MWh, gas 0.45 t per MWh. Solar, wind, hydro and nuclear count as zero while they run.
  • Solar output by hour uses a sunrise-to-sunset curve for Sofia's latitude. Compare it with real output in PVGIS. Bulgaria's yearly mix comes from Ember via Our World in Data.
  • Batteries hold the GWh you set, can charge or discharge at a third of that per hour, and lose 10% of the energy on a round trip.
